# User Personas

## Purpose

This document defines the people Prompt Architect Studio is being built for.

The app should help everyday users who already use AI but struggle to turn rough intent into reliable, reusable prompts.

The product is not strictly for B2B users. Small business, marketing, consulting, and internal work are important use cases, but the broader audience is any nontechnical person who wants better AI outputs without learning prompt engineering.

## Primary Persona 1: The Everyday AI User

### Profile

The everyday AI user uses ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini for practical tasks but does not think of themselves as a prompt engineer.

They may be writing emails, planning a project, summarizing notes, comparing options, preparing content, organizing personal admin, or trying to get a model to understand a rough idea.

### Goals

- Turn messy thoughts into clear AI instructions.
- Get a better first answer without learning frameworks.
- Keep their own voice and intent intact.
- Avoid feeling like AI prompting is a technical skill they have failed to learn.

### Frustrations

- AI gives generic answers.
- The model misses the point.
- They know what they mean, but not how to phrase it for AI.
- Prompt engineering terminology feels intimidating or irrelevant.

### Success Looks Like

- They paste a rough idea and immediately understand what the app will do.
- The generated prompt sounds like a clearer version of their own intent.
- They can copy it into their AI tool and get a useful result on the first try.

## Primary Persona 2: The Small Business Owner

### Profile

The small business owner uses AI to move faster on customer communication, local marketing, hiring, service descriptions, operations, and everyday planning.

### Goals

- Write clearer customer-facing messages.
- Turn scattered notes into practical plans.
- Create useful drafts without hiring extra help for every small task.
- Keep the output approachable and human.

### Frustrations

- AI output can sound too corporate or generic.
- They do not have time to learn prompting systems.
- Their business context is obvious to them but often missing from the prompt.
- They need useful drafts, not theory.

### Success Looks Like

- The app helps them turn a rough business need into a ready-to-run AI prompt.
- The resulting prompt preserves their local, personal, or brand-specific voice.
- They feel more capable using AI for everyday business tasks.

## Primary Persona 3: The Marketing Professional

### Profile

The marketing professional uses AI for positioning, campaign planning, content briefs, competitor research, customer interviews, landing pages, and sales enablement.

### Goals

- Create clearer prompts for strategic work.
- Produce outputs that follow a specific format.
- Keep client or campaign context organized.
- Improve consistency across repeated marketing tasks.

### Frustrations

- AI gives generic marketing answers.
- Important constraints get lost.
- Frameworks are useful, but remembering which one to use takes effort.
- Different models respond differently to the same prompt.

### Success Looks Like

- They paste a rough idea and get a prompt they can immediately run.
- The output prompt feels polished enough to use with clients or internal teams.
- They trust the generated structure enough to reuse it.

## Secondary Persona 4: The Solo Founder

### Profile

The solo founder uses AI to move faster across product, marketing, sales, customer research, and operations.

### Goals

- Turn ideas into useful outputs quickly.
- Avoid rewriting prompts from scratch every time.
- Get better first-draft results from AI tools.
- Use different models without relearning prompting each time.

### Frustrations

- AI outputs are inconsistent.
- Prompts get too long and messy.
- The founder knows the business context, but the model misses the point.
- They do not want a heavy prompt-management platform.

### Success Looks Like

- They feel the prompt captures their intent better than their first draft.
- They come back whenever they need to structure a new task.

## Secondary Persona 5: The Consultant or Agency Operator

### Profile

The consultant or agency operator uses AI across multiple clients, industries, and deliverables.

### Goals

- Standardize prompt quality.
- Reduce repetitive setup work.
- Help junior team members prompt more consistently.
- Build repeatable client workflows.

### Frustrations

- Prompt quality varies across the team.
- Good prompts get lost in chat threads or docs.
- Client context is often incomplete.
- Outputs need to sound professional and structured.

### Success Looks Like

- They can turn a client request into a clear prompt quickly.
- Team members can use shared frameworks.
- The tool becomes a lightweight prompt preparation step before work begins.

## Secondary Persona 6: The Creator or Educator

### Profile

The creator or educator uses AI to plan lessons, explain concepts, create examples, script videos, write newsletters, and build educational content.

### Goals

- Make AI outputs more structured and audience-aware.
- Create prompts that produce reusable content formats.
- Translate rough ideas into lessons, scripts, or explainers.

### Frustrations

- AI output can be bland or generic.
- Tone and audience are often underspecified.
- The creator has to ask several follow-up prompts to get usable material.

### Success Looks Like

- The app helps them specify audience, tone, format, and teaching goal.
- The prompt output reduces revision cycles.

## Secondary Persona 7: The Internal Team Operator

### Profile

The internal operator uses AI for internal memos, summaries, SOPs, planning docs, retrospectives, and cross-functional communication.

### Goals

- Make internal AI work more consistent.
- Convert messy context into useful documents.
- Standardize recurring prompts.

### Frustrations

- Internal prompts often lack enough context.
- Outputs miss constraints or decision criteria.
- AI-generated docs require too much cleanup.

### Success Looks Like

- The app helps turn internal context into clear instructions.
- The final prompt creates structured, professional internal outputs.

## Persona Design Implications

- The interface should not assume technical users.
- The first use should succeed without onboarding.
- The app should show example prompts for common life, small business, creative, and work categories.
- Framework names need plain-language explanations.
- The output should feel useful and human first; professional polish is one possible mode, not the default personality.
- Saved prompt history may become valuable later, especially for consultants and teams.
